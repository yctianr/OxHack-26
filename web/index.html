Which sewage works should be inspected first?

Overflows may only spill once a works is treating its full permitted flow. We screen 53 Thames Water works for spills while treatment ran well below the permit, and spills the weather doesn't explain, so inspections reach avoidable discharges into rivers and wildlife habitats first.

What to request from the operator

    Leads, strongest first

    How a lead is found

    Spilling below the permit. A works must keep treating its flow to full treatment (FFT) while it spills; compliance is judged as 95% of flow readings above 92% of FFT. On a day it spilled for 20+ hours, treatment should have run at FFT all day. We flag those days when daily treated flow was under 80% of FFT, a margin for outlet metering and daily averaging.

    Spills the weather doesn't explain. Oct 2024 to Oct 2026: days the monitor recorded 1+ hour of spilling while two independent models gave under 5% chance from the rain and ground wetness (a statistical model of each overflow, and a physical water balance of each works fitted only to its treated flow, which spills only once the works is at full capacity and never sees a spill record).

    A lead is a reason to look, not evidence of a breach. Daily volumes are measured at the outlet while permits apply at the inlet; the operator's 15-minute flow (MON4) and overflow (MON3) records settle it. Permitted flows come from the Oxford Rivers Improvement Campaign's capacity sheet. Ofwat found capacity and operational issues at over two-thirds of Thames Water works with FFT permits, and Hammond et al. 2021 found a Thames Water works spilling well below its permitted flow.

    How good are the models?

    How to read this

    Overflows release untreated sewage into rivers when sewers are overloaded. Physics model (main): a water balance of each treatment works, hour by hour, fitted only to its daily treated flow. Statistical backup: a per-overflow logistic "bathtub" of rain and wet ground, fitted to spill records; it covers overflows with no treated-flow data and gives the forecast, which stands at midnight with no rain forecast. Scores are on a test year neither model saw.

    River glow shows spilled sewage: each treatment works' daily spill volume from a physics water balance (fitted only to its treated flow; it spills only once the works is at capacity and its storm tank is full), summed downstream and divided by the river length upstream as a stand-in for dilution. Network and pumping-station overflows have no treated-flow data and add nothing. It is a model estimate, not a measurement, and it does not confirm water quality.
